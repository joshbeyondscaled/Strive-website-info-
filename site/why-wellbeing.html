Why wellbeing

Sickness absence is costing your business more than you think.

Absence in the UK is at its highest level in over 15 years, and stress and poor mental health now drive the longest absences. Here's what the official data says, and why prevention is the cheaper option.

The scale

More people are off sick, for longer.

The UK sickness absence rate was 2.0% of working hours in 2025, still above its pre-pandemic level.

Days lost, 2025148.8mWorking days lost to sickness or injury in the UK, an average of 4.4 days per worker. Office for National Statistics, May 2026
Per employee9.4Average days of absence per employee a year, the highest in over 15 years, up from 5.8 in 2022. CIPD and Simplyhealth, September 2025
Out of work2.8mPeople aged 16 to 64 economically inactive because of long-term sickness. Office for National Statistics, 2026
Stress and burnout

Stress causes the longest absences.

Work-related stress, depression and anxiety now account for over half of all work-related ill health in Great Britain, and the trend is still rising.

Workers affected964,000Workers with work-related stress, depression or anxiety in 2024/25, causing 22.1 million lost working days. Health and Safety Executive, November 2025
Per case22.9Average days lost per case of stress, depression or anxiety, the longest of any work-related condition. Health and Safety Executive, November 2025
Under pressure91%Of UK adults experienced high or extreme pressure or stress in the past year; around one in five needed time off as a result. Mental Health UK, January 2025
Long-term absence41%Of organisations name mental ill health as a top cause of long-term absence, more than any other cause. CIPD and Simplyhealth, September 2025
The cost

What it costs employers.

Poor workplace health£85bnA year to UK employers through sickness, staff turnover and lost productivity. Each day of sickness absence costs around £120 in lost profit. Department for Work and Pensions and Department for Business and Trade, November 2025
Poor mental health£51bnA year to UK employers: £24bn from presenteeism, £20bn from staff turnover and £7bn from absence. Deloitte, 2024

Short-term absence now costs from day one.

Since 6 April 2026, Statutory Sick Pay is paid from the first day of sickness, not the fourth, and the lower earnings limit has gone, so part-time and lower-paid staff now qualify too. The rate is £123.25 a week, or 80% of average weekly earnings if lower. Acas, April 2026

On top of sick pay come the costs that don't show on payroll: cover and overtime, missed deadlines and the extra load on the rest of the team.

Long-term absence is the most expensive of all.

Statutory Sick Pay can run for up to 28 weeks, while you cover the role and the work slows down. The longer someone is off, the harder it is to come back, and ill health is now the leading reason people leave the workforce altogether. Department for Work and Pensions and Department for Business and Trade, November 2025

Losing an experienced employee means recruitment, training and months of lost knowledge.

The return

£4.70

Average return for every £1 UK employers invest in supporting their employees' mental health. Deloitte, 2024

Prevention costs less than absence.

Most of the cost of poor health is in people who are still at work but struggling, and in people who leave. That's where early support pays back fastest. Healthier habits, regular coaching and someone checking in help people stay well, spot problems early and stay in work.

  • Structured challenges build the everyday habits behind sleep, energy and resilience.
  • Coaching and check-ins catch stress before it becomes a long absence.
  • On-site physiotherapy, massage and occupational health keep minor problems minor.
  • We measure the change, so you can see the return for yourself.
